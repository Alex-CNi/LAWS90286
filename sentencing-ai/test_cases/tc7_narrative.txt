AGREED SUMMARY OF FACTS

On 5 November 2022 the accused, Natalie Brennan (then aged 34), drank at a friend's barbecue near Ballarat over about six hours. At about 11.50 pm she drove home on the Midland Highway. She held a full Victorian driver licence.

On a straight stretch of road with an 80 km/h limit she drove at about 120 km/h, drifted onto the wrong side of the road and collided head-on with a car driven by Mr Peter Lowe (aged 58), who was driving home from work. Mr Lowe died at the scene.

An evidentiary breath test taken within two hours of the collision recorded a blood alcohol concentration of 0.142 grams per 100 millilitres. The accused accepts that she was intoxicated by alcohol, that it noticeably affected her driving, and that her intoxication significantly contributed to the collision. She was charged with culpable driving causing death, on the basis of gross negligence, and pleaded guilty at the first available opportunity.

ANTECEDENTS AND OTHER MATERIAL

The accused has one prior conviction, in 2017, for driving with a prescribed concentration of alcohol (0.07), for which she was fined and her licence was cancelled for six months. She is a single mother of a 10-year-old son and works as an aged-care worker. She has attended alcohol counselling since the offence and has not drunk alcohol since. She was held on remand for 3 days before being granted bail. There is no psychiatric or psychological report. A pre-sentence report is available, and she consents to a community correction order. Mr Lowe's wife and adult children have made victim impact statements.
