AGREED SUMMARY OF FACTS

At about 2.40 am on Saturday 12 March 2022 the accused, Liam Rourke (then aged 23), was waiting in a taxi queue outside a nightclub in King Street, Melbourne, with two friends. The deceased, Daniel Huynh (aged 31), was further back in the queue. An argument started when the deceased accused the accused's group of pushing in. Words were exchanged, and the deceased turned away to speak to his partner.

As the deceased stood side-on to him, the accused stepped forward and struck him once to the left side of the head with a closed fist. The deceased had not seen the punch coming and did not raise his hands. He fell backwards, unconscious, and the back of his head struck the concrete footpath. The accused left the scene on foot. CCTV footage captured the punch.

The deceased was taken to hospital with a fractured skull and a severe traumatic brain injury. Life support was withdrawn two days later, on 14 March 2022. A forensic pathologist attributed the death to the head injury suffered in the fall.

The accused had been drinking since the early evening. He accepts that he was intoxicated, that the alcohol noticeably affected his behaviour, and that he would not have acted as he did if he had been sober. He handed himself in to police the next afternoon with a lawyer and made full admissions in a recorded interview. He accepts that he intended to punch the deceased to the head and that he knew the deceased was not expecting it.

Before the committal the prosecution gave written notice that it would apply for the mandatory minimum non-parole period on the basis that the manslaughter was committed by a single punch or strike. The accused pleaded guilty to manslaughter after the committal hearing.

ANTECEDENTS AND OTHER MATERIAL

The accused has no prior convictions. He was employed as an apprentice carpenter, and his employer has offered to keep his position open. He has written a letter of apology to the deceased's family, and character references describe the offending as out of character. He has been on bail with a curfew and has not consumed alcohol since the offence. There is no psychiatric or psychological report. The deceased's partner and parents have made victim impact statements describing their grief. A pre-sentence report is available.
