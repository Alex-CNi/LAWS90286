AGREED SUMMARY OF FACTS

On 2 May 2023 paramedics were called to the home of the accused, Michael Halloran (then aged 37), by his sister. She reported that he had stopped taking his anti-psychotic medication and was behaving erratically. Two paramedics in uniform arrived in a marked ambulance.

As one of them, Ms Priya Nair, approached the accused in the kitchen, he picked up a kitchen knife and stabbed her once in the upper chest, saying "you're one of them". Ms Nair suffered a punctured lung, required emergency surgery and was in hospital for nine days. She has not returned to work as a paramedic and has been diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder.

The accused was arrested at the scene and has been in custody since then (290 days at the date of the plea hearing). He pleaded guilty to intentionally causing serious injury after the committal hearing. He accepts that he knew Ms Nair was a paramedic on duty.

EXPERT EVIDENCE (not challenged by the prosecution)

A report of a consultant forensic psychiatrist, Dr Lee, states that:
- the accused has paranoid schizophrenia, diagnosed in 2015;
- at the time of the offence he was acutely psychotic after stopping his medication, and believed the paramedics were agents sent to harm him;
- the psychosis was causally connected to the offence and substantially reduced his capacity to reason and to control his conduct;
- his condition is now stabilised on depot medication;
- imprisonment would be significantly more burdensome for him than for a person in normal health, because of the risk of his condition deteriorating in custody.
Dr Lee recommends ongoing treatment supervised in the community. The accused had not used alcohol or drugs before the offence.

ANTECEDENTS AND OTHER MATERIAL

The accused has three prior convictions for minor offences: two for possessing cannabis (2012) and one for resisting police (2016), all dealt with by fines. He lives with his sister, who supports him. A pre-sentence report assesses him as suitable for a community correction order with treatment and judicial monitoring conditions, and he consents to such an order.
