SUMMARY OF THE FACTS FOUND AT TRIAL

At about 11.15 pm on 20 January 2023 a neighbour called triple zero after hearing shouting and breaking glass at the home of the accused, Craig Doyle (then aged 41), in Frankston North. Two paramedics, in uniform and in a marked ambulance with its lights flashing, arrived before police. One of them, Ms Sarah Kemp (aged 36), went to the front door to check on the occupant's welfare and announced that she was a paramedic.

The accused opened the door holding a claw hammer and struck Ms Kemp repeatedly to the head. Her colleague pulled her away and called for police, who arrested the accused a few minutes later. He made no comment in his police interview. Ms Kemp died in hospital the next morning from her head injuries.

The jury found the accused guilty of murder after a trial in the Supreme Court. It rejected his account that he did not know who was at the door: Ms Kemp was a paramedic on duty, and the accused knew it. He had used methylamphetamine in the days before the offence. A forensic psychiatrist found no major mental illness and no mental impairment bearing on his moral culpability.

VICTIM IMPACT

Ms Kemp's husband, her two children and her colleagues have made victim impact statements describing the loss of a mother, a partner and a colleague, and the fear that paramedics now feel when attending calls at night.

ANTECEDENTS AND OTHER MATERIAL

The accused has prior convictions for assault (2016) and for contravening a family violence intervention order (2019), for which he served a short term of imprisonment. He has been in custody since his arrest (610 days at the plea in mitigation). He has expressed no remorse.
